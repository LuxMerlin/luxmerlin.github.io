# Finally Making a Portfolio
For the longest time, I dismissed the idea of building a portfolio site. I never felt like I truly needed one, and to be honest, I got by just fine without it. But recently, while staring down my usual backlog of half-started ideas (shoutout to ADHD and a folder of 100+ unfinished projects), I decided it was time to bite the bullet and actually put one together.

Instead of just building a static page and calling it a day, I wanted to turn this into a learning opportunity. I challenged myself to pick a completely new framework and get a functional site live in under a week.

I landed on [Astro](https://astro.build/) with MDX.

### Two main reasons sold me on this stack:

    Speed: Astro’s lightweight architecture and developer experience let me spin up a working core site in less than an hour.

    Frictionless Content: MDX lets me write pages and posts directly in Markdown, while Astro handles rendering them out-of-the-box without requiring complex backend plumbing or extra wiring.

## The Experience So Far

Building with Astro turned out to be surprisingly straightforward. It was refreshing to learn a workflow built entirely around a content-first approach. Not having to architect an entire full-stack system from scratch was a huge mental weight lifted—and honestly, a big reason why this project actually made it across the finish line instead of joining the unfinished pile.

Astro’s ability to pull in Markdown documents and automatically wrap them in predefined layouts makes publishing painless. Adding a new blog post or showcasing a new project is now just a matter of dropping a file into a folder, letting the layout do the heavy lifting, and hitting publish.
